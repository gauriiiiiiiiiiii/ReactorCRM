--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS THE PROBLEM?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A company posts something on LinkedIn — say, an announcement about a startup
programme, a new product, or an event. Hundreds of people like it, comment on
it, or share it. These people have ALREADY shown interest. They are warm leads.

The problem: LinkedIn gives you no easy way to collect all those people,
understand who they are, and reach out to them systematically.

That's what this system solves.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW IS IT DONE IN THE INDUSTRY?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There are 3 real ways companies do this today:

--- WAY 1: LinkedIn Sales Navigator (Official, Manual, Expensive) ---

LinkedIn's own paid tool ($99/month). Sales teams use it to browse who
engaged with posts and manually save leads. It integrates with Salesforce
and HubSpot. Problem: you still can't bulk-download post reactors. It's
slow and manual. Good for enterprise sales teams, not for growth hacking.

--- WAY 2: PhantomBuster (Most Popular in Startups) ---

A SaaS tool that automates LinkedIn using YOUR OWN LinkedIn session cookie.
It has ready-made scripts called "Phantoms":
  - "LinkedIn Post Likers Export"  → gives you a CSV of everyone who liked
  - "LinkedIn Post Commenters Export" → gives you commenter names + text

Cost: ~$56/month. Used by thousands of startups and growth teams.
This is the most common practical solution in the industry right now.

--- WAY 3: Clay.com (Modern Full-Stack Approach) ---

The current state-of-the-art for B2B lead generation:
  Step 1: Pull post engagement data (via PhantomBuster or Apify)
  Step 2: Enrich each profile (company, email via Proxycurl or Apollo)
  Step 3: AI writes personalised outreach messages per lead
  Step 4: Auto-push to HubSpot / email sequencer

Cost: $149-$800/month depending on volume. Used by Y Combinator startups
and growth agencies.

--- WHAT ALL OF THEM HAVE IN COMMON ---

Every single one of these tools does the same core thing:
  1. Log into LinkedIn (with a real user's credentials or session)
  2. Visit the post page
  3. Extract the list of people who reacted or commented
  4. Store and present that data in a usable format

The difference is just HOW they log in and HOW polished the UI is.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE TRUTH ABOUT LINKEDIN'S API
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LinkedIn's official API does NOT let you see who liked or commented on
someone else's post. Their API only works for:
  - Your OWN company page posts (LinkedIn Pages API)
  - Your OWN sponsored content (Marketing API)

For everything else, there is no official API. Every tool in the market —
PhantomBuster, Apify, Dux-Soup, Expandi — uses browser automation with a
real LinkedIn user session. This is the only way it can be done.

LinkedIn has sued scrapers before (the hiQ Labs case). The legal grey area
is: using your OWN account to collect data you could manually collect
yourself is much lower risk than mass automated scraping.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE ARE IMPLEMENTING (AND WHY IT WORKS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We are building the same thing PhantomBuster does, but as a self-hosted
web application with a proper dashboard, lead scoring, and CRM features.

Here is exactly what happens when you use our system:

  STEP 1 — You paste a LinkedIn post URL into our web dashboard.

  STEP 2 — Our system opens a real Chromium browser (via Playwright),
           logs into LinkedIn using your credentials from the .env file,
           and navigates to the post — exactly like a human would.

  STEP 3 — It clicks on the reactions list and scrolls through it,
           collecting: name, headline, profile URL, reaction type
           for every person who liked/reacted to the post.

  STEP 4 — It reads every comment on the post, collecting:
           name, headline, profile URL, comment text.

  STEP 5 — Each person gets a LEAD SCORE (0-100) calculated from:
             - Did they comment (high intent) or just like (lower intent)?
             - What did they say in their comment? Keywords like
               "interested", "apply", "how do I join" score higher.
             - Is their job title in our target profile? (founder, CEO,
               investor, MBA student scores higher)
             - Are they a 1st or 2nd connection?

  STEP 6 — All leads are saved to a database. You see them in the
           dashboard sorted by score: Hot (70+), Warm (45-69), Cold (<45).

  STEP 7 — You can filter, search, update their status (contacted /
           qualified / converted), add notes, and export to CSV or Excel.

The browser uses realistic delays between actions (2-5 seconds, randomised)
to behave like a human and avoid LinkedIn's bot detection.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS APPROACH IS RELIABLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Uses a REAL browser, not an API call. LinkedIn can't distinguish it
    from a human browsing manually.

  - Uses YOUR credentials — so it's within your own account's rate limits,
    not a shared IP that LinkedIn has already flagged.

  - Randomised delays — no robotic fixed-interval clicking.

  - If LinkedIn shows a CAPTCHA, set HEADLESS_BROWSER=false in .env,
    watch the browser window, and solve it manually once.

  - The rest of the system (scoring, dashboard, export) is pure Python/Flask
    with no external dependencies — it always works.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE ARE NOT DOING (AND WHY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - We are NOT using the LinkedIn official API because it doesn't support
    this use case for third-party posts.

  - We are NOT buying a third-party data vendor (Proxycurl, Apollo) because
    this assignment requires us to BUILD the system, not just call an API.

  - We are NOT doing mass scraping. One post at a time, human-speed delays,
    with your own account.
